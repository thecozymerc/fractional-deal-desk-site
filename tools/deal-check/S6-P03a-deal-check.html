The Deal Check — 6-Step First-Pass Checklist.

The first pass run on any client deal before it moves — rebuilt from my own working method, not from a template library. For a client’s company, their own house checklist plugs into Step 6 — and if they don’t have one, that is the first finding.

Sample checklist. Client-specific items get filled in per engagement.

Step 1 — The Why

What are we doing? Why? (The why drives the action.)

Step 2 — The Client’s Status

What does the client have right now? What’s the client’s current deal? Is this a renewal or is this brand new? Are they eligible?

Step 3 — The Signal

What do the stakeholders want? (Name each stakeholder. Write the want next to the name.)

Step 4 — Did They Do Their Part

Docs — all here? Info — complete? What’s missing? (Missing docs, missing info — that is the tell.) Is the approval there? (Sometimes the missing piece is the approval.) Reverse check: anything approved that was not supposed to be?

Step 5 — What Is Blocking It

Is the blocker the missing pieces? Or is a policy in the way? If it’s policy: can we bend it — or break it and then recreate it — so we can make more money?

Step 6 — The House Checklist

Run the client’s own required checklist, in full. No house checklist exists? Record that as a finding, not a footnote.

The principle under all six steps: “The big work is reading the process.”

Inputs, logic, limits — How the pass runs.

Inputs: the deal in front of you, worked through the six steps in order. Logic: each step either clears or produces a named finding; a missing item at any step is the finding, not a footnote. Limits: this is a first pass, not an approval — it decides what needs work before a deal moves.

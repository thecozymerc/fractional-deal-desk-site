Tier 1 — Lead Commercial Paper.

The five most central commercial forms, shown first — each with the analyst note: what I check and redline on that kind of paper before it moves.

Simple SaaS Terms of Service

What I check & redline: the subscription metric definition (named users versus usage-based counting) so the bill matches the grant; renewal price-cap escalators and the notice they require; payment timing and late-fee terms; suspension-of-service triggers for non-payment; and warranty remedies kept limited to repair, replacement, or fee remedies rather than open-ended liability.

Customer US Data Processing Addendum (DPA)

What I check & redline: the audit notice window — 5 business days, not same-day access; breach-cost language kept to the contract's own wording — the provider pays notification costs and 1 year of credit monitoring when it is at fault; CCPA/CPRA service-provider certifications; and customer audit rights bounded to documented security inquiries and third-party audit reports unless the law requires more.

Statement of Work (SOW) Form

What I check & redline: acceptance mechanics — a clear written objection window before deliverables are deemed accepted; invoice triggers tied to verifiable deliverable submissions or calendar dates, never to open-ended satisfaction; customer dependencies written into the document, with delay consequences; and scope changes only by formal written change order.

Customer Data Protection Attachment

What I check & redline: security commitments matched to controls that actually exist (audit baselines such as SOC 2 Type II or ISO where they are genuinely held); the breach section kept to the contract's actual wording — the provider pays notification costs and 1 year of credit monitoring when it is at fault; and customer audit access on prior written notice, in business hours — not unrestricted physical audit rights.

Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement

What I check & redline: the definition of Confidential Information, including how oral disclosures get confirmed in writing; the confidentiality term matched to the sensitivity of what is shared, with trade secrets protected without expiry; the standard carve-outs (public knowledge, prior possession, independent development, compelled disclosure with prompt notice); and no non-solicit or non-compete riders smuggled into a confidentiality form.

Tier 2 — Core Enterprise Paper.

The five core supporting enterprise forms — each with the analyst note: what I check and redline on that kind of paper before it moves.

IT Master Services Agreement

What I check & redline: order of precedence between the MSA and its SOWs, so a statement of work cannot quietly rewrite master protections; the aggregate limitation-of-liability formula measured against fees paid; termination-for-convenience exposure; and vendor background IP — the tools and frameworks the vendor brought with it stay the vendor's.

Preview — sample opening. IT Master Services Agreement · Professional Services; Multiple Statements of Work; Ink Signature; Balanced between Customer and Vendor

Consulting Services Agreement

start with the warning printed at the top of the form itself: this paper is not friendly to the customer. That is not a flaw in the sample; it is the lesson the card teaches. Every form in this library favors somebody, and knowing which side a form favors — before you sign it, and before you send it — is the review work. What I check & redline: background IP and methodology — work-for-hire language must not sweep up the consultant's pre-existing templates, frameworks, or tooling; payment milestones tied to deliverable submission dates; expenses billed at cost with documentation; and liability caps that stay mutual and proportionate.

Preview — sample opening. Consulting Service Agreement · Professional Services; Multiple Statements of Work; IP & NDA Terms; Consultant/Vendor-Friendly; Ink Signature

CONSULTING SERVICES AGREEMENT This Consulting Agreement (this “Agreement”) is by and between ______________, an individual and a resident of California (“Consultant”) and ___________, a __________________ whose principal place of business is _______________ (“Company”). This Agreement includes any current or future statement of work specifically referencing this Agreement and executed by each party (any “SoW”), and all such…

Customer IT Master Agreement (buy-side paper)

What I check & redline, wearing the buyer's hat: affirmative deliverable warranties with a duty to fix non-conforming work; vendor indemnification against third-party IP infringement claims; service-level commitments that carry real consequences; and termination rights that do not strand the buyer mid-implementation.

Software License, Customization & Maintenance Agreement

What I check & redline: the boundary between the license grant and the customization work, so custom code does not accidentally transfer ownership of the base product; acceptance review periods for custom milestones; maintenance renewal fee structure locked in advance; and error-correction commitments tied to defined severity levels.

SaaS Terms of Service with Sub-customers

What I check & redline: flow-down — the primary customer must bind its downstream users to the platform's acceptable-use and data rules; insulation — downstream end-users get no direct contractual claims against the vendor; and the vendor's right to suspend an individual sub-customer account that puts the multi-tenant system at risk.

Weighted Decision Scoring

Committees decide by whoever argues loudest, and then the reasons get rewritten afterwards. This tool is the discipline that replaces that: eight criteria with fixed weights, every option scored on the same normalized 0-to-100 scale, one composite per option, and a rank that falls out of the arithmetic instead of the meeting. It implements the documented Weighted Decision Framework — the criteria measure solution effectiveness, efficiency, usability, and satisfaction — and every number in it is sample data.

Provenance & Operating Record: Built from 15+ years in commercial contracts, deal desk, and revenue operations across contracts up to $300M and 3,000+ SKUs under management. This tool implements the documented Weighted Decision Framework; the eight criteria measure solution effectiveness, efficiency, usability, and satisfaction. Directions

Step 1 — Collect the metrics: gather quantitative performance metrics for each option you are evaluating. Step 2 — Capture feedback: collect qualitative user feedback through structured observations and sentiment grouping. Step 3 — Normalize: put every metric on the same 0 to 100 scale so no single scale can dominate, and enter those normalized scores in the yellow INPUT cells. Step 4 — Apply weights: check the weights in the Weight column — they must add up to 100%, and the check line under the table confirms it. Step 5 — Composite: the tool multiplies each score by its weight and adds them up to give one composite score per option. Step 6 — Rank: read the Rank row and the result line — the highest composite wins. Step 7 — Validate: sanity-check the result against the underlying feedback and correlations before you act on it.

Inputs, logic, limits

          
Inputs: the criterion weights and the 0 to 100 scores for Option A, Option B, and Option C. Logic: Composite = SUMPRODUCT of weights and scores for that option; Rank and the highest-composite line are formulas. The weights are 30 / 20 / 15 / 12.5 / 10 / 5 / 2.5 / 5 percent across the eight criteria, and the weight-total line warns in words if they stop adding to 100%. Limits: scores must already be normalized to 0 to 100 before entry, with higher meaning better for every criterion — the tool does not normalize for you. If you change a weight, keep the total at 100% or the composites will not be comparable. And in the workbook's own words: this workbook implements the documented framework from the Weighted Decision Framework document; it is NOT a "50-30-20" tool.

One framework, two decisions.

        
Why fixed shared weights keep evaluation meetings honest

      


      

        

          
The main scoring grid

          
The main sheet scores three generic options against the framework's eight criteria — Overall Satisfaction carries 30% because it is the primary indicator of solution effectiveness; the two correlation criteria and the click counts carry small weights on purpose, so a supporting signal can inform the result without dominating it. Change any weight and the check line under the grid tells you whether the framework still adds to 100% before you trust the composites.

        

        

          
The worked vendor evaluation

          
The worked example points the same eight criteria and weights at a vendor evaluation — Sample Vendor A, B, and C, one sample evaluation team's normalized judgments — so the method can be seen working end to end on a complete decision. Its weights are not typed on that sheet: every Weight cell is a live formula linked to the main sheet, so the example can never drift from the framework. It demonstrates the method on generic sample vendors; it is not a record of any real procurement, and the framework itself is a decision method, not a vendor-scoring product.

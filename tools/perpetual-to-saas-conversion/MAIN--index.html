Perpetual-to-SaaS Conversion Calculator

When a customer is deciding whether to stay on a perpetual license or move to subscription pricing, this calculator compares staying put with three SaaS options, year by year. It shows both the cumulative break-even and the year when the annual run-rate crosses over, with a chart so the shape of the decision is visible.

Provenance & Operating Record: Built from 15+ years in commercial contracts, deal desk, and revenue operations across contracts up to $300M and 3,000+ SKUs under management. All numbers preloaded here are generic sample values for demonstration only — rename any option, adjust seat growth, discount, coupon credits, usage, and contract term in months and days to model a real deal.

How the working calculator runs

Workbook v12 mechanics · 20-year horizon · unrounded term

Below is the working calculator — the workbook's mechanics, live. The names and data are samples where they need to be, but the mechanics are real: change an input, and the tool answers. The formulas stay under the hood. Each SaaS option is priced the real way — a list price per seat per month and a discount give the net PUPM; a starting seat count and a yearly growth rate grow the deal — and each option can also carry a one-time coupon credit and a usage line, priced per use or per token. You can rename each option, too — the page calls it by your name everywhere below. So the page shows both stories at once: what the conversion saves per seat, and why the total can grow as the rollout grows. A seats-by-year table sits under the cost table, so you can watch each option's seats grow year by year — or hold flat, when its growth is set to zero — and a cost-per-seat line divides each option's total by the seats it serves in the final year. In this sample, the third option stays flat while the first two grow.

How to use & Inputs / Logic / Limits

        
Workbook v12 operating instructions and calculation boundaries

      


      

        

          
How to use

          
    
            
  1. Enter the inputs: license, maintenance, licensed seats, the term in months (plus extra days), any Year 1 migration discount; then per option its list price, discount, starting seats and growth, its coupon credit and its usage line. The subscription figures are derived from the seat block, not typed in. Rename any option and everything calls it by your name.
    2. 
            
  2. Read the year-by-year table on a cumulative basis, and the seats-by-year table below it, so a growing option and a flat one compare directly.
    3. 
            
  3. Read the results: the break-even year for each option, the totals at the end of your term, the difference between the paths, and the comparison summary with the chart — the summary shows both views per option: the break-even year (cumulative) and the run-rate crossover year (the first year the option's annual cost is lower than the stay-put annual cost).
    4. 
          

        


        

          
Inputs, logic, limits

          

            Inputs: the stay-put figures (license, maintenance, licensed seats), the term in months plus any extra days, the Year-1 migration discount, and per option its name, list price per seat per month, discount, starting seats, yearly growth, Year-1 coupon credit, and usage line.
          

          

            Logic: staying put costs the license once in Year 1 plus maintenance every year, and covers the licensed seats. A SaaS option is seat-driven: net PUPM = list price per seat × (1 − discount); seats grow each year at the option's growth rate (rounded to whole seats); a year's subscription = that year's seats × net PUPM × 12, with the migration discount and the one-time coupon credit in Year 1 only, plus the usage cost every year. Usage cost for a year = the greater of the committed monthly minimum or the metered usage (units per month / units-per-price × price per unit × (1 − usage discount)), times 12. The migration discount applies to all three options equally. The option names are labels only — they change no math. A growth of 0% keeps an option's seats flat. The term is set in months (plus extra days) — the way contracts are actually written; whole years inside the term read straight off the twenty year columns, and the leftover months and days are charged pro-rata at the next year's cost (days as days/365 of that year). A term past 20 years reads as 20 years; break-even and crossover read in whole years.
          

          

            Limits, stated plainly: every figure is a generic sample value; the model prices the paths side by side and does not judge which path a real customer should take; nothing here is pricing or legal advice.

Cloud Commit & Usage Playbook.

A playbook is not a document — it is the order decisions get made in. This one runs the way an enterprise cloud deal desk runs: governance first (a discount matrix that says who may approve what), the renewal gate second (no discount clears if it shrinks the renewal), and the value levers third (the commit, sized against real usage, with the waste showing). Three tabs, all of it live, all of it sample data.

Directions: Start on the Discount Matrix tab. The matrix is not a picture — the thresholds and the approver names are inputs, so re-tier it (raise what Sales may approve, rename a tier) and every route in the workbook moves. Set a discount in the Route-a-discount block and the matrix names its approver. The Renewal Check tab takes the current TCV, the renewal's list value, and the discount being asked: it returns the renewal TCV, the verdict under the ratchet rule, and the deepest discount that would still grow the renewal — with the approver for that counter-play read back through the matrix. The Commit & Usage tab sizes a spend-based commitment against expected usage: what the commitment covers, what bills at full price above it, what you save, and what you paid for and never used.

Inputs, logic, limits

Inputs: the matrix's band thresholds and approver names, the discount asked, the current TCV and renewal list value, and the commit block (expected usage at list, the monthly commitment, the commit discount, the term in months). Logic: the band chain and the nested-IF routing are the workbook's own formulas; the ratchet verdict compares the discounted renewal TCV against the current TCV, and the counter-play discount is the exact depth at which the two are equal, capped at zero; the commit tab covers usage up to the list value the commitment buys at its discount, bills the rest at full price, and counts any commitment left unused as paid waste. Limits, stated plainly: the thresholds and approver names are sample values, not any company's approval policy; the commit mechanics are a simplified sample model of a spend-based commitment (real programs differ by product in eligibility, true-ups, and how discounts land); and nothing here is pricing, legal, or financial advice — it is a playbook's skeleton, made touchable.

Where this playbook comes from.

This workbook is an original sample, built in November 2026 for this site. Its structure follows how enterprise cloud deal desks actually organize their playbooks — the pattern that falls out of the real thing: the approval rules sit in front of the plays, not inside them; the standard path is short and the exceptions carry the weight; and every play ends in a routing line, a named owner for the next step, rather than an explanation. The commit mechanics are the public ones — committed-use discounts are documented openly by the cloud providers (Google Cloud's published CUD documentation is the reference here) and by practitioner bodies like the FinOps Foundation. No internal system, codename, knowledge-base text, real threshold, figure, or SKU from any employer's playbook is in this file; what carried over is the grammar, which is the part worth showing.

Three choices in the build are deliberate. First, the matrix is the input: on most sites a discount matrix is a screenshot, and a screenshot cannot be argued with — here a visitor can re-tier it and watch the routes change, which is what a matrix is for. Second, the ratchet computes its own counter-play: a blocked renewal does not just say no, it prices the deepest discount that still grows the TCV and names who would approve it, which turns a refusal into a negotiation position. Third, the waste line: commitment tools usually show coverage and savings and stop; this one also shows the commitment dollars paid and never used, because that is the number that decides whether the commit was sized right.

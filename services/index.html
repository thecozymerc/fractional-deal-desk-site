05 — Services & Pricing

What You Can Hire Me For

Most companies try to fix deal friction by buying or configuring software. Software cannot fix unclear business rules, bad intake, or a process nobody owns.

Before you automate the quote-to-cash process, make it worth automating.

Phase 1 — Diagnose the operation

Contract & Revenue Diagnostic — $6,500 fixed fee, 2–4 weeks.

A deep look at the deal desk, pricing guardrails, approval bottlenecks, quote-to-contract workflow, active contract risks, and the cost of leaving the current system in place. You get a maturity score, workflow maps, a risk matrix, an executive readout, and a prioritized 90-day roadmap.

Contracts-only option — $5,000 fixed fee.

A focused audit of active customer and vendor agreements for hidden liabilities, dangerous renewals, unfavorable terms, and margin leaks.

Phase 2 — Build the fix

Execution & Implementation Builds — $4,500 to $25,000, typically 3–8 weeks.

I build the commercial playbooks, discount rules, approval workflows, contract processes, data structures, and tools identified in the diagnostic. If you already know what is broken, Phase 2 can also be scoped directly.

Phase 3 — Stay in the operation

Fractional Execution & Advisory — $4,000, $7,500, or $12,500 per month.

Ongoing deal governance, live redlining, exception handling, negotiation support, reporting, and cross-functional alignment. Retainers begin with a 90-day commitment, then continue month to month on 30 days’ notice.

The hero offer: Embedded Commercial Redliner & Deal Desk

$7,500 per month.

Direct help on live buyer paper, with a 24-hour target turnaround on complex redlines. This is for companies hitting procurement walls and needing someone who understands both the seller’s margin problem and the buyer’s procurement playbook.

  • Live redlining of customer contracts to protect margin, payment terms, and liability.
  • Negotiation support when procurement pushes for long payment terms, cash refunds, open-ended liability, custom data terms, or hard price caps.
  • Downtime remedies handled in the right order: continuity credits first, capped credits against future invoices second, with payouts tied to chronic failure rather than one bad day.
  • Concessions traded for something real — upfront payment, a longer term, or another commitment that matters.
  • Pre-negotiation coaching for account executives, plus help structuring non-standard deals that do not fit the drop-down menu.

How engagements are billed

Fixed-fee projects are typically billed 50% up front and 50% on delivery. Retainers are billed on the first of the month.

Introductory discounts and Phase 1 continuity credits can roll into the next Phase 2 build or Phase 3 retainer when agreed up front in writing. One credit applies per engagement, credits do not stack, and they reduce future invoices rather than being paid back as cash refunds.
